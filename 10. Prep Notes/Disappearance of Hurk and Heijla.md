### **Act 1: The Vanishing**

**Setup:**  
The session begins with the party waking up the morning after leaving the note for the Myriad, ready to set sail. However, they quickly realize that Hurk and Heijla are missing. Their beds are unmade, their belongings are gone, and there’s no sign of a struggle. It’s as if they simply left.

If they ask around the inn, the concierge tells them that Hurk and Heijla checked out early in the morning. They left together, saying they had a change of heart and went to reconsider the Myriad’s offer.

**Clues & Misdirection:**

- If they investigate, the innkeeper provides a **forged letter** supposedly written by Hurk, stating that he and Heijla decided to take the Myriad’s deal and that it was _better they go separate ways before things get too complicated_.
- If they check the docks, the dockworkers confirm that **two individuals matching their descriptions boarded a ship at dawn**—a ship that has since left port heading **somewhere unknown** (it could be a ghost ship arranged by the Myriad or even a random merchant vessel that has no idea of its passengers' fate).
- Any attempt to track them leads to **dead ends**—Hurk and Heijla’s disappearance was carefully orchestrated.

---

### **Act 2: The Warning**

As the party processes their disappearance, the Myriad’s “Salesman” makes his final move:

- A messenger (a street urchin, an unassuming commoner, or a scared local) approaches with **a sealed note bearing the Myriad’s insignia**.
- The letter is elegantly written, yet carries an unmistakable threat:

> _“Regretfully, your friends have made a choice. A shame, truly. But let this serve as a reminder—the Myriad does not take kindly to refusals. Consider this our final warning. Walk away. Speak no more of us. Or the next bodies found in the swamp will be yours.”_

If the players attempt to find the messenger, they will lead them into an ambush—or flee into the crowd, disappearing effortlessly.

---

### **Act 3: The Decision Point**

By this point, the players are left with a choice:

1. **Try to find Hurk and Heijla** (which will lead to dead ends).
2. **Respect the warning and move forward with their journey.**

To further discourage them from searching, **Captain Dorian Maldrake** (or another wise NPC) will heavily advise against retaliation. He knows the Myriad is not to be trifled with and that fighting them now is suicide.

If needed, the **Myriad could arrange a final "distraction" to force them out of the city**, such as:

- Framing them for a **crime** (forcing them to flee).
- **A public execution of an “informant”**—a Myriad member who “talked too much” about them, making it clear what happens to those who cross them.

---

### **Future Possibilities (If the Players Ever Want to Find Them Again)**

- **Hurk and Heijla could return on their own**—as either escapees or brainwashed members of the Myriad.
- **The party hears rumors** months later about captives in a Myriad stronghold, and their friends could be among them.
- **If the original players ever return**, the Myriad may have forced them into **debt, servitude, or exile**, creating new personal arcs.

---

### **Outcome**

By making the abduction **clean, professional, and intimidating**, it ensures that the players don’t waste time searching for them but also leaves the door open for their return.
