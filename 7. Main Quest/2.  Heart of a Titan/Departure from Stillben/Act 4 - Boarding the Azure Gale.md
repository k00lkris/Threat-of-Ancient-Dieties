# Session 15 — Act 4: Boarding the Azure Gale

**Location:** Stillben waterfront, aboard the *Azure Gale*  
**Time:** Departure morning; exact hour determined at the table  
**Encounter:** Exploration, social interaction, transition, optional complications  
**Previous:** [[Act 3 - Choices Before Departure]]  
**If the party sails:** [[Act 4 - Journey at Sea]]  
**Captain:** [[Captain Dorian Maldrake]]

> [!important] Running this act
> **Only run the boarding sequence if the characters actually come to the ship.** This is a living port scene, not a cinematic that overrides a decision to investigate Hurk and Heijla. Captain Maldrake's ship follows the departure clock established in [[Act 3 - Choices Before Departure]]. The party should hear a clear boarding warning before missing passage.

## DM overview

Stillben is busy regardless of what the Reapers decide. Cargo is arriving, sailors are checking lines and provisions, and other vessels are working the waterfront. The *Azure Gale* is preparing for an ordinary commercial sailing into increasingly unfamiliar waters. The captain has not secretly enlisted in the Myriad's schemes.

The immediate goals are to:
- Give the players a tangible, memorable introduction to the ship and its established crew.
- Provide a brief, open opportunity to speak with Maldrake about Hurk, Heijla, the Myriad, and departure arrangements.
- Establish practical shipboard boundaries **without** imposing a new surprise cost or contract.
- Move naturally from port to sea if the Reapers choose to sail.
- Preserve the possibility that they instead return to the city, miss departure, or make another arrangement.

> [!note] Known vs. proposed
> The *Azure Gale*, Maldrake, the arranged passage, and the five named crew NPCs are already in the vault. The sensory details, briefing, and optional complications below are **new scene preparation**. The ship's exact dimensions, full crew complement, fare paid, boarding time, armament, and cargo manifest are **not yet established**; avoid asserting them as past facts.

## Scene 1: The morning waterfront

*Use this when the party arrives at the quay. If they approach under pursuit, shorten the introduction and address the threat first.*

### Read aloud

> Stillben's waterfront is already wide awake. Porters call over creaking wheels, merchants argue across stacks of bundled cargo, and gulls circle above a forest of masts. The damp smell of river water, salt, timber, and tar hangs in the air.
>
> Beyond the bustle, the *Azure Gale* rests alongside the quay. Her crew work with the practiced rhythm of people who have loaded a hundred ships before: a line drawn taut here, a crate guided into the hold there, a sailor high in the rigging checking the canvas.
>
> A woman on the deck calls for someone to clear the gangplank. Nearby, an older man traces a finger along a chart while a younger sailor swings confidently from one line to another.
>
> The ship is nearly ready. Whether you are ready to leave Stillben is another question.

**Ask:** "What do you do as you approach the ship?"

### What the players can notice

| Attention or approach | What they learn |
|---|---|
| Watch crew preparations | Everyone has duties and the crew behaves professionally; no roll required |
| Inspect safety or seamanship | **DC 12 Perception or relevant tool proficiency:** the crew is carrying out sensible final checks; not a guarantee against hidden trouble |
| Ask how long until sailing | The gangway watch tells them the current departure stage and directs them to Mara or Maldrake |
| Look for anyone observing them | Use established Myriad information from [[Act 2 - The Myriad's Warning]] and character actions. **Do not invent a guaranteed tail or compulsory ambush** |
| Ask about missing companions | Maldrake does not know their fate; the dockside investigation in [[Shadows Over Stillben]] remains available until departure |

**No gatekeeping roll:** The party has arranged passage. Don't make boarding contingent on an arbitrary Persuasion check.

## Scene 2: Meeting the crew

Use only as many introductions as the pacing permits. Let conversations arise from work rather than lining five NPCs up for speeches.

| NPC | First sight | Optional interaction |
|---|---|---|
| [[Mara Voss]] — first mate | Directing cargo loading and boarding | "Keep the gangplank clear. If you're sailing, I'll need your names and where you'll stow your gear." |
| [[Thalen Reed]] — navigator | Comparing his chart to the morning light and current | Can discuss the proposed sailing route, winds, and why a 7–10-day estimate is only an estimate |
| [[Bram Copperkettle]] — cook | Supervising baskets and galley stores | Offers food, friendly gossip, or a chance to learn about life at sea |
| [[Pip Ashfall]] — deckhand | Working the rigging above the quay | Curious about the Reapers' equipment or magic; may offer to show them a safe place on deck |
| [[Garran Holt]] — shipwright | Inspecting planking, seams, and a hatch cover | Can discuss maintenance, tools, and salvaging materials with Notos |
| [[Captain Dorian Maldrake]] | Final check near the quarterdeck | Deals with passage, safety, and departure decisions |

**DM reminder:** NPCs are not mandatory quest givers, undercover enemies, or preselected casualties. Their `statblock` entries are on their individual pages if sudden danger requires mechanics. Unnamed sailors handle normal operations.

### Useful social checks (only when an outcome is uncertain)

- **DC 12 Persuasion:** A busy crew member makes time to explain a routine matter or allow a brief supervised look around.
- **DC 13 Insight:** Mara's sharp manner is professional urgency, not hostility.
- **DC 12 Investigation or relevant tool proficiency:** Garran or Thalen can show a curious character an interesting practical feature of the ship.
- **No check:** Speaking politely, learning names, asking basic safety questions, and making ordinary introductions.

These checks are suggestions; reward appropriate roleplay and relevant proficiencies.

## Scene 3: The captain's briefing

Maldrake is strict but fair. He addresses the party directly once they approach him or Mara calls him over. Adjust his speech if the party has already shared information about the missing companions.

### Read aloud / dialogue

> Captain Maldrake looks up from a folded chart.
>
> "You're the travelers bound for the Shattered Isles. Good. The crew handles the ship; you follow their instructions when we're working the deck. Keep dangerous experiments clear of the rigging, don't interfere with the watches, and tell my first mate if you've a problem."
>
> He glances toward the quay.
>
> "If there's unfinished business ashore, now is the time to say so. Once we cast off, I won't turn back on a whim."

**Questions he can answer, without inventing earlier arrangements:**

- **When do we sail?** State the deadline communicated in play, consistent with the [[Act 3 - Choices Before Departure|departure clock]]. No previously established absolute hour.
- **How long?** The working expectation is **7–10 days** toward the newly proposed Shattered Isles position, subject to seas, weather, and chosen route. This is a planning estimate, not a guaranteed schedule.
- **Can we help?** Yes, with consent and supervision. Deck work is not required of booked passengers. See [[Act 4 - Journey at Sea]] for optional shipboard skill activities; adapt mechanics later as needed.
- **Can we store equipment or conduct alchemy?** Routine luggage can be stowed as directed. Fire, fumes, volatile mixtures, and open flames require arrangements with the captain and cook or shipwright; don't ban Notos's crafting outright.
- **What about Hurk and Heijla?** Maldrake is sympathetic but cannot independently locate them. He may allow a limited delay if circumstances permit, never an indefinite one.
- **How much did we agree to pay?** **Do not demand a new fare** here. Resolve only if the players raise an actual unresolved payment question; prior draft ship-choice notes mention 200 gp/person, but Maldrake's dedicated page explicitly states the agreed fare was not fixed in played history.

### Brief ship rules

1. The captain commands the ship; Mara directs routine deck operations.
2. Crew-only working areas require permission, especially while cargo is moving.
3. No uncontrolled fire, dangerous alchemical experiments, or weapons practice near lines, stores, and passengers.
4. In an emergency, obey immediate safety directions; the characters can still choose how to help.

These are reasonable policies, not an excuse to confiscate equipment or negate class features.

## Scene 4: Final call — a decision, not a cutscene

When the fictional departure clock reaches **boarding call**, have Mara give a clear final announcement to all passengers.

> Mara raises her voice above the creak of the rigging.
>
> "Final boarding for the *Azure Gale*! If you're sailing with us, get aboard now. Once those lines are cast, we're gone."

**Pause and ask:** "Is there anything else you want to do in Stillben before the ship leaves?"

| Player choice | Immediate adjudication |
|---|---|
| Board | The crew assists with ordinary gear. Proceed to [[#Scene 5 Cast off]]. |
| Ask Maldrake for a brief delay | Refer to his established principles and [[Act 3 - Choices Before Departure]]. Negotiate a clear cutoff; do not promise indefinite waiting. |
| Return to investigate Hurk and Heijla | Permit it. The ship may sail without them if time runs out. |
| Attempt to intercept a watcher or courier | Run the relevant portion of [[Act 2 - The Myriad's Warning]]; let the clock matter but avoid forced outcomes. |
| Reveal an immediate credible threat to the vessel | Maldrake evaluates the evidence and protects his crew; a real emergency might justify a delay. |
| Refuse passage or miss departure | Use [[Act 3 - Choices Before Departure#7. Miss the Azure Gale]]. The Heart of a Titan quest remains accessible by other means. |

> [!warning] Player agency
> **Don't move the ship out of port mid-conversation simply to end the scene.** Announce the impending deadline and give a meaningful final choice. Equally, don't freeze the tide and ship indefinitely if they knowingly stay ashore.

## Scene 5: Cast off

*Use only if the Reapers are aboard and the captain decides to depart.*

### Read aloud

> A sharp command rings across the deck. Lines are loosened, hauled in, and coiled away. The gangplank is pulled aboard, and the last voices from the quay fade beneath the snap of canvas catching wind.
>
> Stillben begins to slip astern. Warehouses, crowded berths, and the last familiar outlines of the city draw slowly together behind you.
>
> Ahead lies open water—and beyond it, somewhere across the Lucidian Ocean, the Shattered Isles.
>
> For the first time in days, the noise of the city gives way to the creak of timber and the steady rush of water along the hull.

**After the read-aloud:** Ask where each character goes and what they do during the first hour. Offer choices organically: meet the crew, unpack, study charts, check equipment, write notes about Hurk and Heijla, or watch Stillben recede. No encounter is compulsory.

### First hour at sea: quick prompts

- **Mara:** Who is staying clear of the crew's working lines? Who volunteers to help?
- **Thalen:** Does anyone ask about the course or the proposed 651.7-mile measurement?
- **Bram:** Who comes by the galley when food is served?
- **Pip:** Who is willing to answer a curious sailor's questions?
- **Garran:** Does Notos ask about repairs, wood, salvage, or a safe workspace?

Don't run every prompt unless the players engage. If they have a strong immediate agenda, follow it.

## Optional brief complications — pick at most one if pacing benefits

These are **not guaranteed** and should never contradict a choice already made.

### A. A crate slips

A shifting load knocks a crate loose near the gangway. **DC 12 Athletics or Acrobatics** to stabilize or evade it; creative use of magic, a shouted warning, or crew teamwork may resolve it automatically. On failure, a minor spill delays loading briefly and embarrasses the porter; don't derail the voyage with major damage from one check.

**Purpose:** A little shipboard action and a first impression of Mara's leadership.

### B. News from the waterfront

A dock runner calls for a sailor to collect a message. The note concerns routine cargo or navigation, **not automatically the Myriad**. If the party already attracted Myriad attention and prior acts support it, this can instead deliver a plausible reaction from that faction.

**Purpose:** Keep the city active while respecting earlier decisions.

### C. Unfinished personal business

A player wants to leave a message for an ally, document the disappearance, or arrange for Waterwatch follow-up. Let them use a dock runner or speak to an existing official if time allows; set reasonable costs and delivery expectations in play.

**Purpose:** Provide a meaningful emotional transition without forcing the party to abandon the main quest.

## DM reference: important boundaries

- The *Azure Gale* and Maldrake **do not know** the truth of Hurk and Heijla's abduction automatically.
- A staged departure and forged letters remain the Myriad's scheme. Do not convert a routine cargo handler into an omniscient Myriad spy.
- Missing the ship is an allowed consequence, **not** a campaign-ending failure.
- The voyage is provisionally **7–10 days**, based on the proposed Shattered Isles site about **651.7 miles from Stillben**; this is a planning measurement, not exact canonical navigation.
- The [[The Crimson Abyss|Crimson Abyss]] and Salty Dogs are genuine deadly ghosts, but **do not introduce the ship automatically on leaving port**. Any nautical encounter belongs to later voyage planning.
- Leave the specific curse mechanism, Hurk's potential role in breaking it, and ultimate fate of either missing companion unresolved.
