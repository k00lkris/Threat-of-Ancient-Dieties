# Session 15 — Act 3: Choices Before Departure

> [!important] How to use this chapter
> This is a **branch reference**, not seven encounters to run in order. Use only the sections that respond to what the Reapers actually do after [[Shadows Over Stillben]] and [[Act 2 - The Myriad's Warning]]. **The Myriad has a plan, but the characters retain agency.** The *Azure Gale* keeps its own schedule: **9:30 AM departure**, communicated in advance.

**Place:** Stillben, principally the inn, streets, Waterwatch offices, and docks  
**Time:** Morning of the planned voyage  
**Next destination if the Reapers sail:** the Shattered Isles aboard the *Azure Gale*  
**Key NPC:** [[Captain Dorian Maldrake]]

## At a glance: likely player decisions

| The Reapers… | Run this section | Main consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Accept the farewell letters | [[#1. Accept the letters]] | Sail with an unresolved disappearance |
| Suspect the letters are forged | [[#2. Question the letters]] | Uncover leads, but not automatic answers |
| Pursue dockside witnesses | [[#3. Investigate the docks]] | Identify an irregular departure |
| Confront the Myriad | [[#4. Confront the Myriad]] | Intimidation, negotiation, pursuit, or open conflict |
| Ask the Waterwatch for help | [[#5. Seek the Waterwatch]] | An official report and possible inquiry |
| Ask Maldrake to wait | [[#6. Request a delay]] | Negotiate limited grace or choose to stay |
| Miss or refuse the ship | [[#7. Miss the Azure Gale]] | Find a different route; the campaign continues |

> [!note] Existing mechanics
> **Act 1** already contains the primary letter, room, concierge, and dockside investigations; **Act 2** already covers the warning, courier, and Myriad tactics. Use those established checks and clues first. The additional leads below are **optional DM expansions**—they do not retroactively change what happened.

---

## 1. Accept the letters

If the Reapers accept Hurk's and Heijla's apparent decisions, let them grieve, complain, or continue preparations without manufacturing reasons to investigate. Stillben is waking up: vendors, porters, and sailors are at work. The *Azure Gale* finishes taking on supplies.

**Maldrake's response, if asked:**

> "I'm sorry to hear your companions have chosen another path. But I've a ship to command and a tide to catch. If you're sailing with me, be aboard before we cast off."

**Outcome:** They board as planned, if they choose. Hurk and Heijla remain missing and the Myriad retains its secret for now. Do not punish the players for believing convincing forgeries.

## 2. Question the letters

If a character recalls that Hurk could not read the cursed book in Whitestone, **confirm that memory without a roll**. A polished handwritten letter attributed to Hurk is suspicious, but he might have dictated it. This is an investigative opening, not instant proof.

Use the checks and distinctions in [[Shadows Over Stillben]]: a **DC 25 Investigation** check can establish professional forgery through physical analysis, while comparing genuine samples or asking about the writing can produce different leads. Do not treat a failed roll as confirmation that the letters are genuine.

**Optional supplemental witness:** A hallway attendant recalls soft footsteps before sunrise but no familiar voices. A **DC 13 Persuasion** check, or a calm and credible conversation, may elicit this memory. The attendant cannot identify anyone.

**Outcome:** The party can investigate further, report a suspected crime, or leave for the ship. Identifying a forgery does not automatically reveal the captives' location.

## 3. Investigate the docks

Run the existing dockworkers and ship-departure clues in [[Shadows Over Stillben]] first. The Reapers can ask about two travelers matching their friends' descriptions and the early-morning vessel they reportedly boarded.

**Optional supplemental evidence:** With time to examine berth records, **DC 18 Investigation** reveals an incomplete departure entry that raises questions about the ship's paperwork. This is evidence of an irregularity, **not proof that Hurk and Heijla were aboard**, nor proof of where the real captives are.

**Outcome:** A lead can be recorded for future investigation. If players propose effective ways to follow the vessel, adjudicate them fairly rather than declaring the trail impossible. A prolonged pursuit may mean missing the *Azure Gale*.

## 4. Confront the Myriad

The Salesman wants the Reapers gone and the Clasp assassination offer forgotten. He would prefer to avoid a public fight that attracts the Waterwatch. The Myriad is dangerous but **not omniscient**.

A low-level contact can deny involvement:

> "Your friends made their decisions. We don't control every traveler who passes through Stillben. I'd suggest you concern yourselves with your own business."

A **DC 15 Insight** check may indicate careful evasion but does not establish who holds the captives. If conflict breaks out, use the existing [[Act 2 - The Myriad's Warning]] guidance and appropriate Myriad stat blocks, including the limits on what ordinary operatives know. Let the players choose diplomacy, threats, surveillance, pursuit, capture, withdrawal, or combat.

**Outcome:** The party may obtain an actual lead, attract authorities, make an enemy, or decide to disengage. Capturing a rank-and-file operative does not automatically reveal the operation's leadership or prisoner location; equally, a clever interrogation should not be dismissed without considering what that NPC plausibly knows.

## 5. Seek the Waterwatch

A Waterwatch officer takes a missing-person report and requests concrete details: the letters, witness descriptions, and any forgery evidence.

> "Two missing travelers and a pair of suspicious letters? That's worth looking into. But if you're accusing the Myriad, I'll need something stronger than a feeling."

With credible evidence, the officer can begin inquiries or request copies of records. Local officials have their own caseload and political pressures; an immediate rescue is **not** promised.

**Outcome:** The report creates a possible later contact or follow-up. The Reapers can leave the authorities to investigate, remain to help, or pursue their own inquiry.

## 6. Request a delay

Maldrake is a professional former naval officer who operates a disciplined ship. He can be sympathetic without sacrificing his obligations to passengers, crew, cargo, or the sailing conditions.

| Request | Likely response |
|---|---|
| A few minutes to gather belongings | Allows it if preparations permit |
| Brief time to speak with authorities | May grant a limited grace period before casting off |
| Several hours for an uncertain investigation | Usually refuses |
| Wait until Hurk is found | Refuses to hold the voyage indefinitely |
| Return after already sailing | Requires a compelling reason and real negotiation |

**Roleplay:** "If you mean to sail, be aboard when I call it. I won't risk everyone on this ship for a promise I cannot keep."

**Outcome:** If the party wants to keep investigating, let that choice matter. Be clear about their likely deadline before it passes.

## 7. Miss the Azure Gale

If the party remains in Stillben or simply decides not to sail, **the *Azure Gale* departs on schedule** unless the characters achieved a credible delay.

The Heart of a Titan quest does not vanish. The characters might secure another ship, seek an arcane means of travel, wait for passage, or continue pursuing Hurk and Heijla. The alternative captains in [[Act 3 (2) - Choosing a Ship]] are **possibilities, not guaranteed to be in port**.

**Outcome:** Their travel logistics and the timing of the Shattered Isles expedition change. The world and opposing factions continue to act; do not punish this choice with an automatic campaign dead end.

## DM pacing: the departure clock

Advance these **fictional stages** when meaningful time passes, not after every die roll. Tell players plainly when departure is close. **The ship is scheduled to cast off at 9:30 AM.**

- [ ] **Morning preparations.** Discovery of Hurk and Heijla's absence; dockworkers load cargo.
- [ ] **Final loading.** Crew secures supplies; passengers begin boarding.
- [ ] **Boarding call.** Maldrake expects the travelers aboard. One last limited opportunity for urgent business.
- [ ] **Casting off (9:30 AM).** The *Azure Gale* sails unless reasonably delayed.

> [!tip] Running the scene
> The Myriad's pressure should not consume every street corner of Stillben. Most citizens are simply working. Tension comes from **the uncertainty of the disappearance and the ticking sailing schedule**, not from a mandatory sequence of attacks.

## Continuity and unresolved truths

- **DM fact:** The Myriad orchestrated the disappearance, employed decoys, and planted convincing forged letters. See [[Shadows Over Stillben]].
- **Unresolved:** Hurk's and Heijla's current location and eventual fate. Do not invent a guaranteed rescue or an inviolable barrier to finding them.
- **Future hook:** Hurk's former crew aboard the ghost ship *Crimson Abyss* may later connect his survival to an unknown curse. This is **not** a clue the players automatically receive in Stillben.
- **Preparation principle:** These are short branches that react to player decisions, **not** full side quests or a railroad.
