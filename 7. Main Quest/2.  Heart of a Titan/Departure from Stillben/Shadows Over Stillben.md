# Session 15 — Shadows Over Stillben

## Act 1: The Empty Beds

**Location:** Stillben, Inner Town — The Party's Inn  
**Time:** Early Morning, Day of Departure  
**Encounter Type:** Social / Mystery / Investigation  
**Primary Faction:** The Myriad  
**Quest:** [[Heart of a Titan Summary]]

---

## Encounter Overview

The party awakens in their rented suite on the morning of their scheduled departure aboard the _Azure Gale_, captained by [[Captain Dorian Maldrake]].

Two companions, [[Hurk Dingleback]] and [[Heijla Valkyr]], are missing. Their belongings are gone, their beds are empty, and two letters have been left behind.

The letters claim that both adventurers have reconsidered the Myriad's offer and decided to remain in Stillben.

What the surviving party members don't know is that **the letters are expertly forged, and their companions have been abducted by the Myriad**.

### Encounter Objectives

- Establish Hurk and Heijla's disappearance.
    
- Provide a believable explanation for their apparent departure.
    
- Allow the characters to investigate the letters and surrounding circumstances.
    
- Reinforce the Myriad's influence throughout Stillben.
    
- Give the party meaningful choices without forcing them into an investigation.
    
- Establish a natural transition toward either further investigation or departure aboard the _Azure Gale_.
    

---

## DM Only — What Really Happened

Following the party's refusal of the assassination contract, the Myriad orchestrated the disappearance of Hurk and Heijla.

The criminal organization had already been observing the adventurers and gathering information about their individual interests.

The Myriad knew:

- **Hurk** was searching for information about his former pirate crew, the Salty Dogs, and the fate of the _Crimson Abyss_.
    
- **Heijla** was searching for arcane knowledge and clues concerning her forgotten past.
    
- The party had arranged passage aboard the _Azure Gale_ and planned to depart Stillben.
    
- The party had rejected the Myriad's offer to assassinate a Clasp captain.
    

The Myriad commissioned a master forger to create convincing farewell letters using these personal motivations.

The organization intends to make the disappearance appear voluntary, discourage pursuit, and demonstrate the consequences of rejecting its influence.

**Important:** The whereabouts of Hurk and Heijla remain unknown. This encounter does not require the party to rescue them or discover their ultimate fate.

# Scene 1: An Unusually Quiet Morning

### Read-Aloud Text

> The first pale rays of morning sunlight creep through the windows of your suite, illuminating the polished wooden furniture and the remnants of last night's preparations.
> 
> Outside, Stillben has already begun to stir. Merchants shout from the streets below, wagon wheels rattle against cobblestones, and somewhere in the distance, the familiar cries of seabirds announce another busy morning at the docks.
> 
> But something is different.
> 
> The room is quieter than usual.
> 
> Hurk's bed is empty. So is Heijla's.
> 
> Their traveling belongings are gone, and there is no indication that either has returned.
> 
> On the writing desk, resting beneath a small brass candleholder, lie two folded pieces of parchment.
> 
> One bears Hurk's name.
> 
> The other, Heijla's.

**DM Instructions:**

Pause here and allow the players to react.

Do not immediately reveal the letters' contents. Allow the party to decide whether to read them, inspect the room, or investigate the disappearance.

The scene should feel unsettling without immediately suggesting foul play.

# Scene 2: The Farewell Letters

If the party reads the letters, provide the following information.

## Handout A — Hurk's Letter

> Friends,
> 
> I've been doing some thinking about that offer we got last night. I know we all agreed to walk away, but I reckon things are a bit different for me.
> 
> Stillben's a port town, and there's more sailors and old sea dogs here than anywhere we've been. If I'm ever going to find out what happened to the Salty Dogs and the Crimson Abyss, this might be my best chance.
> 
> The Myriad has connections. They say they can help me find answers, and I'm willing to take that chance.
> 
> Don't go getting yourselves into trouble looking for me. I've made my choice, and you've got your own journey ahead.
> 
> Fair winds,
> 
> Hurk

### DM Notes — Hurk's Letter

The forger has carefully incorporated information about Hurk's former pirate crew and his search for the _Crimson Abyss_.

These details are intended to make his departure believable to the remaining companions.

**Potential inconsistency: Hurk's literacy.**

During the party's earlier encounter with the cursed book in Vornelith Blackthorn's tomb, Hurk demonstrated an inability to read.

If the party recognizes that Hurk may also be unable to write, the handwritten letter becomes immediately suspicious.

Do not require a difficult ability check for players who independently remember and identify this inconsistency.

The Myriad may claim that Hurk dictated the letter to someone else, but this explanation should not automatically resolve the party's suspicions.

---

## Handout B — Heijla's Letter

> My friends,
> 
> I know that my decision may come as a surprise, particularly after our conversation last night, but I have chosen to remain in Stillben.
> 
> The Myriad has offered me access to arcane knowledge and magical research that I might never find elsewhere. They have resources, connections, and records that may help me understand more about my abilities and the life I cannot remember.
> 
> Hurk has decided to stay as well, although for reasons of his own. Neither of us wishes to interfere with your journey to the Shattered Isles.
> 
> Please don't mistake our departure for a betrayal. We've simply found different paths to follow.
> 
> I wish you all well. Perhaps our roads will cross again.
> 
> Heijla

### DM Notes — Heijla's Letter

Heijla's interests make the Myriad's supposed offer believable.

Her desire to understand her arcane abilities and forgotten history provides a plausible reason to remain in Stillben.

The letter deliberately reassures the party that the departure is voluntary and unrelated to their refusal of the Myriad's assassination contract.

Both letters discourage the adventurers from pursuing their companions.

**This similarity is intentional.** The Myriad wants the surviving characters to continue their journey rather than investigate.

# Scene 3: Investigating the Letters

The letters were created by a master forger employed by the Myriad.

The handwriting, parchment, ink, and stylistic details have been carefully selected to imitate authentic correspondence.

Identifying the documents as forgeries through physical examination alone is extremely difficult.

## Ability Checks

|Check|DC|Result|
|---|---|---|
|Intelligence (Investigation)|12|Both letters were deliberately positioned together on the writing desk.|
|Wisdom (Insight)|15|The letters provide plausible explanations, but both discourage further contact or investigation.|
|Intelligence (Investigation), with Calligrapher's Supplies proficiency|20|The documents show signs of unusually careful preparation, raising questions about their authenticity.|
|Intelligence (Investigation)|**25**|Subtle inconsistencies in lettering, writing pressure, and stroke patterns reveal that the letters are professionally forged.|
|Intelligence (Investigation), comparing authentic handwriting samples|**23**|Small inconsistencies between known writing samples and the letters strongly indicate forgery.|

### Recommended Forgery DC: 25

A DC 25 Investigation check represents a **Very Hard** task under D&D 5e difficulty guidelines.

A successful check establishes that the letters are forged.

It does not automatically identify the Myriad as the forger's employer.

### Successful Investigation — Read-Aloud

> As you study the letters more carefully, something begins to trouble you.
> 
> The handwriting is remarkably convincing. Almost too convincing.
> 
> Certain letters repeat with an unnatural consistency. The pressure of the pen follows patterns that suggest deliberate imitation rather than ordinary writing.
> 
> Whoever created these documents understood their craft extraordinarily well.
> 
> But not well enough.
> 
> You're holding forgeries.

### Failed Investigation

> The parchment appears ordinary, the handwriting familiar, and the ink consistent with the writing materials commonly available throughout Stillben.
> 
> Nothing immediately suggests that the letters are anything other than genuine farewell messages.

**DM Note:** A failed check does not establish authenticity. It only means the character cannot identify evidence of forgery.

### Additional Investigation Methods

**Detect Magic:** The letters were created using mundane techniques and possess no magical aura.

**Identify:** The spell does not determine the authenticity of ordinary handwritten documents.

**Insight:** May reveal unusual phrasing or inconsistencies with the characters' known behavior, but cannot conclusively establish who wrote the letters.

**Comparison:** Characters who possess authentic examples of Hurk's or Heijla's handwriting may use them during their investigation.

**Player Deduction:** Reward players who identify contradictions through roleplay and memory rather than relying exclusively on ability checks.

# Scene 4: Investigating the Suite

If the party searches the suite, describe the following.

### Read-Aloud Text

> The room shows no obvious evidence of violence.
> 
> The furniture remains undisturbed. There are no overturned chairs, broken glasses, or bloodstains.
> 
> Hurk's and Heijla's traveling belongings are missing, and their beds appear to have been vacated during the night.
> 
> Whatever happened here, it left remarkably little evidence behind.

### Investigation Checks

|Check|DC|Result|
|---|---|---|
|Investigation|10|Neither companion left behind their usual traveling equipment.|
|Investigation|15|The room shows no signs of an ordinary struggle.|
|Investigation|20|Faint evidence suggests the room was deliberately straightened or cleaned after the pair departed.|

**DM Note:** The final result is a possible clue, not conclusive proof of abduction.

A sufficiently resourceful party may discover additional information through creative use of spells, equipment, or questioning.

Do not invent arbitrary magical protections solely to prevent a successful investigation.

# Scene 5: Questioning the Concierge

If the party approaches the front desk, the concierge reports that Hurk and Heijla departed before sunrise.

The concierge believes this account to be true.

The Myriad arranged for individuals resembling the missing companions to be seen leaving the inn.

### Read-Aloud Text

> The concierge looks up from a large ledger as you approach.
> 
> "Good morning! I trust your accommodations were satisfactory?"
> 
> At the mention of Hurk and Heijla, the concierge pauses.
> 
> "Ah, yes. Your companions departed rather early, I'm afraid. The large gentleman and the young lady."
> 
> "They mentioned a change of plans. Something about an opportunity here in Stillben."
> 
> "I assumed the letters explained everything. They were quite insistent that you not be disturbed."
> 
> The concierge looks between you.
> 
> "Is something wrong?"

## What the Concierge Knows

- Two individuals resembling Hurk and Heijla left the inn before sunrise.
    
- They appeared to depart voluntarily.
    
- The concierge was instructed not to disturb the remaining companions.
    
- The concierge knows nothing about an abduction.
    
- The concierge does not know the letters are forged.
    

### Ability Checks

|Check|DC|Result|
|---|---|---|
|Wisdom (Insight)|12|The concierge appears sincere and genuinely believes the account.|
|Charisma (Persuasion)|13|The concierge provides a detailed description of the departure.|
|Intelligence (Investigation)|15|The inn's records support an early checkout, although the information could have been manipulated.|
|Charisma (Intimidation)|15|The concierge becomes frightened and cooperative but has no further knowledge of the missing companions.|

### If Asked for Descriptions

The concierge describes two travelers wearing clothing similar to Hurk's and Heijla's.

If the party presses for details, the concierge may reveal that neither traveler spoke very much.

This is an intentional weakness in the Myriad's deception.

# Scene 6: The Docks

If the party investigates the docks, several dockworkers recall seeing two travelers matching Hurk and Heijla's descriptions board a vessel shortly before sunrise.

The ship has already departed.

### Read-Aloud Text

> Stillben's docks are alive with activity.
> 
> Sailors haul cargo aboard waiting vessels while merchants argue over manifests and shipping costs.
> 
> The smell of saltwater, fish, and rotting vegetation hangs heavily in the air.
> 
> When you describe your missing companions, one of the dockworkers scratches his beard thoughtfully.
> 
> "Big fellow and a young woman, you say? Aye, think I saw 'em."
> 
> He gestures toward an empty berth.
> 
> "Boarded a ship just before dawn. She's long gone now."
> 
> "Where to? Couldn't tell you. Ships come and go all the time around here."

### Dockside Investigation

|Check|DC|Result|
|---|---|---|
|Persuasion|12|A dockworker confirms the apparent boarding.|
|Investigation|15|The vessel left unusually early, before normal harbor activity began.|
|Persuasion or payment|17|A sailor recalls the vessel's description or identifying markings.|
|Insight|14|The witnesses seem sincere, though none can confirm the travelers' identities.|

### DM Information

The Myriad staged the apparent departure to support its forged letters.

The individuals observed boarding the ship were not necessarily Hurk and Heijla.

The actual location of the missing characters remains unknown.

**Important:** The party should be allowed to follow credible leads. Do not automatically invalidate clever investigation or divination. If they uncover the deception, allow that discovery to influence the Myriad's subsequent actions.

This encounter has no predetermined player outcome.

The Myriad has acted, and the party must decide how to respond.

# Scene 8: Transition to the Next Situation

The Myriad intends to ensure the remaining adventurers understand that rejecting its offer carries consequences.

However, the timing and nature of its response should depend on the party's behavior.

### If the Party Accepts the Letters

The adventurers can continue toward the docks.

A Myriad messenger may approach them during their departure preparations, delivering a subtle warning.

### If the Party Discovers the Forgery

The Myriad becomes aware that its deception may have failed.

A representative may attempt to intimidate the party or discourage further investigation.

### If the Party Confronts the Myriad

The organization denies responsibility.

Depending on the party's approach, the encounter could become a negotiation, intimidation attempt, or confrontation.

### If the Party Pursues Other Leads

Allow the investigation to unfold logically.

The Myriad remains a powerful criminal organization with considerable resources, but it is not omniscient or infallible.

### If the Party Leaves Stillben

Allow them to depart.

The consequences of their refusal and the disappearance of their companions may remain unresolved, becoming part of the campaign's ongoing history.

---

## End of Act 1

**Intended Transition:** Situation 2 — The Myriad's Warning, or Situation 3 — Departure aboard the _Azure Gale_, depending on player choices.

### DM Reminder

The purpose of this encounter is not to force the surviving party members into a predetermined storyline.

Hurk and Heijla's disappearance is an established event, but the surviving characters determine their own response.

The Myriad's deception should be convincing but discoverable.

The players may accept the letters, investigate the disappearance, confront the organization, or continue toward the Shattered Isles.

**Prepare the consequences. Let the players write the story.**
