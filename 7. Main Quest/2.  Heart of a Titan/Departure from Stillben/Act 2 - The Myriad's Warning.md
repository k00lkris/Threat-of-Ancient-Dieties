# Session 15 — Act 2: The Myriad's Warning

**Location:** Stillben — Inn, streets, markets, or docks (determined by the party's choices)  
**Time:** Morning of the *Azure Gale*'s scheduled departure  
**Encounter Type:** Social / Threat / Optional Investigation or Combat  
**Primary Faction:** The Myriad  
**Previous Act:** [[Shadows Over Stillben]]  
**Next Act:** Departure aboard the *Azure Gale* (to be developed)  
**Party:** Level 6 (confirm surviving party size before tuning combat)

---

## Encounter Overview

After the discovery that [[Hurk Dingleback]] and [[Heijla Valkyr]] have apparently left, the Myriad attempts to close the matter. A courier carries a sealed warning intended to discourage the surviving Reapers from challenging the organization or lingering in Stillben.

**This is a situation, not a cutscene.** The party may accept the letters from Act 1, expose them as forgeries, interrogate witnesses, leave for the docks, or launch an immediate counterattack. The warning reaches them **only if the Myriad has a plausible opportunity to deliver it**. They can also leave town without ever receiving it.

> [!important] Design principle
> The Myriad's plan is fixed; the party's response is not. Do not force a confrontation, a chase, a kidnapping rescue, or a missed ship departure.

### Encounter Objectives

- Demonstrate that the Myriad's offer and the party's refusal had consequences.
- Preserve uncertainty about the fate of Hurk and Heijla without making evidence impossible to find.
- Put a living faction with limits, resources, and motives in front of the party.
- Support diplomacy, deception, pursuit, investigation, nonlethal violence, combat, and withdrawal.
- Hand off naturally to the docks and [[Captain Dorian Maldrake]], **if the party chooses to sail**.

---

## DM Only — The Myriad's Position

The Myriad's representative (the unnamed **Salesman** who presented the Clasp assassination contract in Session 14) is attempting to reassert control after the party's note: **"Not interested."**

The Myriad arranged the abduction and apparent departure of Hurk and Heijla, employing decoys and forged letters as detailed in [[Shadows Over Stillben]]. The Salesman wants to:

1. Prevent the Reapers from challenging Myriad authority in Stillben.
2. Avoid attracting the Waterwatch or provoking a wider fight while the Clasp–Myriad rivalry is active.
3. Ensure the Reapers leave town, preferably believing their companions departed willingly.
4. Leave open the possibility of exploiting these adventurers in the future.

**Limitations:** The Salesman is not omniscient. A courier does not automatically know where the party is. A captured foot soldier does not know the location of the captives. A witness can be truthful and mistaken. The Myriad can be outmaneuvered.

**Unresolved DM fact:** The ultimate location and fate of Hurk and Heijla have not been fixed by this chapter. Their immediate whereabouts must remain consistent with the staged departure, but do not invent unbreakable protections if the party pursues effective leads.

### What the Salesman Knows

- The party refused the contract to assassinate a local Clasp captain.
- The Myriad arranged the false appearance of a voluntary departure.
- The *Azure Gale* is the party's expected route out of Stillben.
- The Salesman has **not** personally observed every choice the party made in Act 1.

### What the Courier Knows

- A contact paid them to deliver a sealed note to the described adventurers.
- The delivery location or identifying details supplied by that contact.
- **Not** the details of the abduction, the forgery, or the identities of senior Myriad operatives.

Choose the courier's identity according to the scene: a nervous dock runner, a street hawker, or an adult errand carrier. The messenger is **not automatically a combatant or a willing accomplice**.

# Scene 1 — The Delivery

Use the entry that fits what happened in Act 1. Do not play all versions.

| Act 1 outcome | Where the warning can appear | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Party accepts the letters | On the road to the docks, while preparing to sail | Tone is cool and final; the Myriad prefers them to leave |
| Party suspects or proves forgery | After they question witnesses, **if** a lookout reports it | Warning is sharper; further questioning creates risk for the Myriad |
| Party confronts Myriad contacts | Through a representative in person or a courier after withdrawal | The faction must respond to the actual conversation, not a preset script |
| Party seeks [[Captain Renaud Varn]] | Only if the messenger has a believable way to find them | Any approach near the Watch risks exposing the courier |
| Party departs immediately, avoids contact | No delivery required | The Salesman must live with an unfinished warning |

### Read Aloud — Street or Dockside Delivery

> Stillben is already awake around you. Cart wheels scrape over uneven stones, dockhands argue over cargo, and the air smells of wet timber and river mud.
>
> Someone approaches from the moving crowd—not with a weapon drawn, but with an envelope held out at arm's length.
>
> "For you. Told I'd find you here."
>
> The messenger's eyes flick toward the street behind them.
>
> "I was paid to hand it over. That's all."

Allow players to interrupt, question, restrain, bribe, or ignore the messenger **before** the delivery is complete.

**If they refuse the letter:** The courier will not insist. They may leave it on a nearby crate if safe, or withdraw with it. There is no magical guarantee the message reaches the party.

# Scene 2 — The Sealed Warning

A modest wax seal bears an emblem associated with the Myriad. An adventurer who saw the Salesman's identifying mark or correspondence may recognize it without a check; otherwise, **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** or an appropriate background connection identifies the likely association. This check establishes a connection, **not** that the contents are truthful.

### Handout — The Myriad's Warning

> To our recent guests,
>
> Your companions have chosen a different path. You would be wise to respect their decision.
>
> Our offer was made in good faith. Your refusal was noted.
>
> Stillben is a city of arrangements. Some are offered once.
>
> Leave on your scheduled vessel. Do not inquire further into our affairs, and we shall consider the matter closed.
>
> Continue to interfere, and you may discover how few friends you have in this city.
>
> **— An Interested Party**

> [!note] The contradiction is intentional
> The letter simultaneously claims the missing companions made a voluntary choice and threatens retaliation for the party's refusal. Players may notice this on their own. Do **not** require an Insight roll to understand the literal words.

**Optional escalation:** If you want the Salesman's voice to be explicitly crueler, use the original prep-note threat ("the next bodies found in the swamp will be yours") from [[Disappearance of Hurk and Heijla]]. This draft's default is restrained intimidation to preserve plausible deniability.

### Reading the Messenger

| Player approach | Suggested check | What they learn |
|---|---|---|
| Ask who paid for delivery | No check if calmly asked | Courier met a hooded adult at a public place, received payment and description |
| Persuade them to recall details | DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) | Clothing, voice, approximate meeting time, direction the contact departed |
| Assess whether they are lying | DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) | Courier is frightened and seems sincere; this doesn't authenticate their employer's claims |
| Bribe for useful details | 1–5 gp suggested, no mandatory roll | Courier willingly shares what they actually observed |
| Threaten or harm them | Resolve normally | Courier may flee or cooperate; bystanders may alert the Waterwatch |
| Follow their route backward | DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) or Intelligence (Investigation), if viable | A meeting place or witness; **not** an automatic path to the abductees |

**Failure-forward:** Unsuccessful checks cost time, create a mistaken impression, or draw attention; they do not erase the basic fact that the courier was hired to deliver the message.

# Scene 3 — The Party Responds

Choose a branch according to the players, then adjudicate unexpected plans using the faction's established motives.

## Branch A — Ignore the Warning / Board the Azure Gale

The Myriad will not risk a public brawl merely to force the party to read a note. If they leave, allow the departure sequence to proceed.

**Possible consequence:** A lookout reports their exit. The Salesman considers the immediate objective fulfilled. Their friends' disappearance remains an unresolved mystery, **not a failed quest**.

**Transition:** Departure aboard the *Azure Gale*.

## Branch B — Question the Courier or Investigate the Seal

The courier knows the delivery contact, not the leadership. Let evidence lead to real places and people.

**Optional lead:** A market stall owner remembers an adult purchasing wax and envelopes matching those used by the Myriad's local runners. This suggests a distribution method, **not proof of the abduction**.

The party may pursue the lead, contact the Waterwatch, interrogate the seller, or return to the docks.

**Transition:** Continued Stillben investigation, negotiation, or departure.

## Branch C — Demand a Meeting with the Salesman

The Salesman prefers a controlled conversation in a public location with exits and witnesses. He can agree to speak **if the party's approach or leverage makes a meeting worthwhile**; he does not magically appear whenever summoned.

### Read Aloud — The Salesman's Response

> "I offered you an opportunity. You declined. That should have been the end of our business."
>
> He adjusts his cuffs, looking past you toward the crowded street.
>
> "You seem determined to make it something more."

**His position:** Publicly, the companions left of their own accord. Privately, the party's persistence makes them inconvenient. He will not confess to abduction simply because a character rolls high on Persuasion.

**Negotiation prompts and outcomes**

| The party does… | Salesman's plausible response |
|---|---|
| Shows forged-letter evidence | Questions the reliability of the evidence; attempts to learn how much they know |
| Threatens to expose him | Tests whether they have witnesses or documents; may propose mutual withdrawal |
| Offers information or a favor | May listen, but does not automatically restore the assassination contract or release captives |
| Demands Hurk and Heijla back | Denies holding them; his reaction may betray concern at their specific knowledge |
| Asks for safe passage | Offers noninterference while they leave, but his word is not independently verified |
| Attacks him in public | Retreats, calls guards or allies if present; bystanders scatter and the Watch may respond |

**Suggested social checks:** DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) for guarded evasiveness; DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion) for a limited concession (e.g., ending overt harassment while the ship sails); DC 18 Charisma (Intimidation) to unsettle him if backed by credible leverage. Checks change what he *chooses to say or do*, not what is true.

**Transition:** Docks, Waterwatch, or further Myriad investigation.

## Branch D — Follow or Seize a Myriad Operative

Use [[Myriad Foot Soldier]] as the **existing** Myriad street enforcer. The soldier can attempt to flee, bargain, or surrender. They do not carry a map to an unseen prison.

**Useful, limited intelligence for a captured soldier:** A courier meeting spot, watch shift, nearby safe door, or nickname of a low-ranking supervisor. Pick one that fits the scene. The party can act on it if desired.

### Optional Combat — Street Enforcers

**Only use combat when fiction supports it**, such as an attempted arrest of a lookout, an assault on a guarded meeting point, or a Myriad decision to silence an exposed operative.

- **Opposition:** 2–3 × [[Myriad Foot Soldier]], CR 1 each.
- **Terrain:** Crates, narrow passages, wet cobblestones, civilian traffic; cover and retreat routes.
- **Enemy objective:** Delay, escape with evidence, or protect an informant—not fight to the death.
- **Party objective:** Determine this from the players' actions: capture, escape, recover evidence, or survive.
- **Difficulty:** For level 6 characters, these are generally low-threat opponents; verify party size and resources before treating them as a balanced combat. Do not add an unprepared elite operative to manufacture difficulty.

> [!info] Quick-add enemies
> Open [[Myriad Foot Soldier]] and click the **+** icon in the rendered statblock to add instances to Obsidian's Initiative Tracker. This workflow was tested for multiple foot soldiers.

**If combat ends:** Resolve arrests, escape, captured witnesses, injuries, and public consequences based on where the fight actually occurred. The Waterwatch need not instantly appear in an empty alley, but violence in a crowded market should attract attention.

## Branch E — Involve Captain Renaud Varn

[[Captain Renaud Varn]] previously offered help with the counterfeit papers and crooked Waterwatch guards in exchange for the party's cooperation. If the party returns with concerns about their missing companions, he listens.

- **Evidence of a forged letter:** Grounds for questions and an investigation, not an automatic raid.
- **Only a threatening note:** Gives Varn a reason to warn about the Myriad but does not reveal the captives' location.
- **Report of a crime or violent confrontation:** He must respond according to duty, public safety, and what witnesses can verify.
- **The ship's departure:** Varn cannot promise that a law-enforcement inquiry will finish before sailing.

**Transition:** Assist Varn, keep investigating, or leave the matter with him and depart.

## Branch F — Attempt Magical Investigation

Adjudicate the precise spell the players use. A mundane forged note is not magically detectable by default. Do not grant divination an invented absolute failure, nor allow a spell to exceed its stated scope without a reason.

If magic establishes a specific location or credible direction, **follow that information and improvise a meaningful lead**. This may extend the adventure beyond the prepared dock departure.

**Transition:** Consequences of the spell's actual result; no predetermined end.

# Scene 4 — The Myriad Reacts

Use this table as a private reaction guide **after** a meaningful player action, rather than advancing a clock simply because a scene number has been reached.

| What the Myriad learns | Reasonable response | What it avoids |
|---|---|---|
| Party boards the ship | Watches departure; stands down for now | Publicly attacking departing passengers without cause |
| Party questions the courier | May monitor known questioning sites | Instantly knowing every question asked |
| Party proves documents forged | Guards communications, alters meeting arrangements | Revealing the captives just to taunt the party |
| Party contacts Varn | Reduces visible activity near Watch | Treating Watch as entirely compromised without established evidence |
| Party assaults enforcers | Pulls back exposed personnel; considers retaliation or law-enforcement pressure | Sending endless reinforcements |
| Party gains leverage | Negotiates, misdirects, or retreats | Automatically surrendering all secrets |

### Escalation Rule

A first warning should remain **one warning** unless the party deliberately escalates. Follow-on retaliation must result from concrete Myriad intelligence, available personnel, and credible risk—not from a requirement to push the party out of town.

# Scene 5 — Departure Pressure (Not a Railroad)

The *Azure Gale* is scheduled to sail. Captain [[Captain Dorian Maldrake]] manages a real vessel with obligations beyond the party.

If the players spend time investigating, tell them plainly when the departure becomes urgent through harbor bells, crew notices, or a runner from the ship. **Do not silently declare they missed departure.**

If they delay:

- Maldrake may insist on a firm boarding cutoff, set when the party asks or when the schedule becomes relevant.
- He may depart without them if their actions genuinely consume the window.
- They may negotiate a delay, find alternative passage, or remain in Stillben.

Their decision—not the chapter outline—determines the route into Act 3.

## End of Act 2

**Suggested transition:** If the party elects to leave, begin preparations aboard the *Azure Gale*. If they stay, continue the faction situation using the established clues and NPC motivations.

> [!tip] DM Reminder
> The Myriad's objective is to control risk and preserve its power, not to perform a scripted villain speech. The Reapers can accept a threat, turn it into a lead, ignore it, bargain, or fight. Follow their choices.
